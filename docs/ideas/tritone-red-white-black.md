# Tritone Red, White and Black

> Crush any footage into three flat colours, black, white and one hard red, and let the beat decide which tone gets the red.

![A red wall with white and black lines on it](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1548511545-bb2a65ba2762?w=1400)
*Similar look, not the original work. Photo: [Claudio Schwarz](https://unsplash.com/photos/a-red-wall-with-white-and-black-lines-on-it-Uja-TBfFpz4) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/license).*

## The idea

Video goes in, a screen-printed poster comes out. Three flat fields, no greys, no gradients, no texture inside a field. Faces turn into stencils. Hands become silhouettes with a red mid-tone band. Smoke and feedback become hard-edged blobs that never stop changing shape. The borders between the fields are the drawing, and they crawl wherever film grain or sensor noise crosses a threshold.

The palette is the old Constructivist one: red, white and black. El Lissitzky used it in 1919, Kraftwerk used it on a record sleeve in 1978, and Barbara Kruger built a career on it. The technique underneath is plain image processing: reduce to luminance, crush the contrast, map the result to three colours with a threshold, a gradient map or a LUT.

**Footage it suits:** high contrast and plenty of motion. Faces with side light, hands on black, type, geometry, feedback. Flat, low-contrast footage turns to mush. As the last effect on a mixed bus it also pulls unrelated clips into one visual world.

**Is "tritone" a word?** Yes, twice, and the meanings are unrelated. In print, a duotone layers a contrasting halftone colour over another, traditionally black plus a second ink such as blue, yellow, brown or red ([Wikipedia: Duotone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duotone)). Tritones (three inks) and quadtones (four) use the same Photoshop dialog ([O'Reilly](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/real-world-print/9780321637598/9780321637598_ch09lev2sec13.html)). Print tritones keep continuous tones. This look is harder: three flat output colours only. In music, a tritone is an interval of six semitones, exactly half an octave, nicknamed *diabolus in musica* ([Wikipedia: Tritone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritone)). A fun overlap for a book about sound and picture, but not the same thing.

## Origins and references

The look has no single origin. These are the reference points, arty ones first.

- **Kraftwerk, *The Man-Machine* (1978).** The V&A describes a limited palette of red, black and white, with the band in red shirts and black ties on a staircase and angled text, inspired by El Lissitzky ([V&A](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O1192920/the-man-machine-record-cover/)). Karl Klefisch did the artwork; the back cover adapts a graphic from Lissitzky's children's book *About Two Squares* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man-Machine)).
- **El Lissitzky, *Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge* (1919).** The reference image for the whole idea: a red wedge cutting into a white circle, a lithographic Bolshevik propaganda poster. Three flat colours and one diagonal gesture ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_the_Whites_with_the_Red_Wedge)).
- **Barbara Kruger.** Black-and-white found photographs with white-on-red captions in Futura Bold Oblique or Helvetica Ultra Condensed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Kruger)). The model for a "text slams in on the beat" layer.
- **Theodore Ushev, *Tower Bawher* (2005).** A three-minute abstract animated short, a fast tour of Constructivist art with nods to Vertov, the Stenberg brothers, Rodchenko, Lissitzky and Popova ([NFB](https://www.nfb.ca/film/tower_bawher/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_Bawher)). The motion reference: how Constructivist geometry moves.
- **Lissitzky echoes on sleeves.** Ryuichi Sakamoto *B-2 Unit* (1980), The Wake *Something Outside* (1983), Franz Ferdinand *This Fffire* (2004) ([John Coulthart](https://www.johncoulthart.com/feuilleton/2014/08/18/el-lissitzky-record-covers)). Franz Ferdinand's 2003 identity by Matthew Cooper freely references Rodchenko and Lissitzky ([Fonts In Use](https://www.fontsinuse.com/uses/17163/franz-ferdinand-franz-ferdinand-andamp-single)).
- ***Sin City* (2005).** Mostly black and white with colour on selected objects ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sin_City_(film))). The spot-colour cousin, not strictly three colours.

No verifiable red/white/black VJ or AV set turned up in the research. The owner's original set is the best live reference.

## Visual language

- **Flat fields.** Screen print, stencil, propaganda poster. No gradients.
- **Black dominates.** Red is the accent, white is the light. Keep red under about 35 % of the frame.
- **The red band carries the form.** It is the "middle grey" between shadow and light. Move it to the shadows or the highlights and the same image reads differently.
- **Constructivist shapes.** Diagonals, wedges, circles and big type match the palette.
- **Contour flicker.** Edges crawl where noise crosses a threshold. The grain becomes strobing lines.
- **Hue is the mood.** Poster red `#E4160C`, vermilion `#FF3B12`, crimson `#B5001F`, hot pink `#FF1F6B` for a short Kruger moment. Black stays `#000000`. White is a warm paper `#F2EFE8`, so the fields do not blind the room.
- **Rhythm by switching, not fading.** The effect itself does not move. Colours jump: red changes band, black and white swap, red drops out and returns on the kick.

## Code design

The design rule: **exactly three output colours, decided last.** The quantiser is the final step before the output. No blur, bloom, scaling or compression may follow it, or new pinks and dark reds creep in. The only exception is a deliberate `edge` softness.

The core is one pass. Take luma (Rec.709 weights), apply gamma and a crush curve around a pivot of 0.5, then split into three bands with two thresholds `A < B`. Map the bands to black, red and white. With edge softness `s = 0` you get exactly three colours. With `s > 0` you get thin ink-spread seams. Two small controls handle the beat flips: `red_pos` (red in shadows, mids or highlights) and `invert` (black and white swap), giving all six permutations. A `red off` gate turns the image pure black and white. Optional extras: a pre-blur, hysteresis against chatter (keep last frame's band per pixel), a rotating wedge mask that decides where red is allowed, and a black keyline on band borders.

=== "Resolume Wire"
    Two routes.

    **Path A, native: a 3D LUT.** Resolume's LUT effect loads `.cube` files only and can sit on a clip, layer, group or composition, with opacity and blend mode ([Resolume: LUT](https://resolume.com/support/de/lut)). Generate the file with a short script. It must be a 3D LUT, since a 1D LUT cannot turn one luma value into a coloured output. Size 33 is standard, 65 gives crisper edges. For each node compute luma, crush it, and output by band: below A (0.30) black, up to B (0.62) red `#E4160C`, above that white `#F2EFE8`. Keep B minus A at least three grid steps, or the interpolation produces grey. Build a bank of eight files: red in mids, shadows, highlights, inverted, pure black and white, and three hue variants. On the composition, put Blur (optional), then Brightness and Contrast as your live threshold controls, then the LUT at 100 % opacity. Below 100 % the video shows through and the promise of three colours is broken.

    **Path B, in Wire.** Either a LUT-bank patch (a LUT 3D Resource node with the bank as a collection, an index picks the file, a beat counter modulo 3 steps it), or an ISF patch for full control: the ISF node runs the quantiser shader and shows its inputs from the JSON header ([Resolume: ISF](https://resolume.com/support/en/isf)). Inputs: `low_cut`, `high_cut`, `edge`, `crush`, `gamma`, `hold`, `red_hue`, `red_pos`, `invert`, `red_off`, `outline`, plus the colours. Whether persistent buffers (needed for hysteresis) run in Wire is not documented. Test it; if they fail, set `hold` to 0 and raise the blur to 2 px.

=== "Unreal Engine 5.7"
    A Post Process Material on a Post Process Volume (Infinite Extent on) or on the CineCamera ([Epic: Post Process Materials](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/post-process-materials-in-unreal-engine)). Domain Post Process, Blendable Location After Tonemapping, output Emissive Color only.

    Node chain: `SceneTexture: PostProcessInput0` into `Dot` with the Rec.709 weights, then `Power` for gamma, then the crush curve, then two `SmoothStep` nodes for the thresholds and two `Lerp` nodes to mix the three colours. The red slot is a Vector Parameter, or a `HueShift` driven by `RedHue / 360`. Skip hysteresis (it needs a render-target ping-pong) and use a 1 to 2 px blur instead.

    Make the thresholds stable: manual exposure, bloom 0, lens flares off, film grain 0, vignette 0, low or no motion blur. All of these move the luma under your thresholds.

    Remote Control: put the parameters in a Material Parameter Collection `MPC_Tritone`, write them from an exposed Blueprint actor, and bind OSC addresses (`/tritone/low`, `/tritone/high`, `/tritone/hue`, `/tritone/edge`, `/tritone/redpos`) and MIDI CCs in a Remote Control Preset. Send the result to Resolume over Spout or NDI. Quantise in only one place, either in UE or in Resolume, not both.

=== "GODOT"
    Godot 4, 2D. Show the source (`VideoStreamPlayer`, a `SubViewport` with a 3D scene, or a camera feed) in a `TextureRect`. Put a full-rect `ColorRect` on top with a `canvas_item` shader that reads `hint_screen_texture` and runs the quantiser. Uniforms with range hints: `low_cut`, `high_cut`, `edge`, `crush`, `red_hue`, `red_pos`, `invert`, `red_off`, `outline`, two `source_color` colours and an optional `mask_tex`. For hysteresis, render the pass into a `SubViewport` with Clear Mode Never and feed last frame's texture back in; skip it if you do not need it. Set uniforms from GDScript with `set_shader_parameter()`, driven by `InputEventMIDI` (via `OS.open_midi_inputs()`) and an OSC addon if needed.

## On stage

**Place in the chain:** last. On the composition or a master group, so every source below shares one three-colour world. Feedback goes beneath the quantiser, never after it, since feedback through a blur creates new in-between colours. After the quantiser: no bloom, glow, grain or sharpening. Want grain? Add noise before it, where it turns into contour flicker. The one thing above it is the Kruger caption: a Text Block, white type on a red bar in exactly the live red hex.

**Audio reactivity**

| band | drives | amount |
|------|--------|--------|
| Kick, 40 to 120 Hz | both thresholds up, the image ducks toward black | +0.06 to +0.10, attack 5 ms, release 150 ms |
| Kick (alternative) | `red off` gate opens, red appears | hold 1/8 note |
| Snare / clap | `invert` flip | max one per beat |
| Hi-hats | `high_cut` jitter | ±0.02, keep it small |
| Long-term RMS | `gamma` | 0.8 loud, 1.3 quiet |

Flip `red_pos` and `invert` from the tempo-locked BeatStep Pro, not from audio. Transients can double-trigger and a wrong colour flip is very visible.

**MIDI**

- **KeyStep 37 knobs:** `low_cut`, `high_cut`, `red_hue`, `edge`, `crush`.
- **BeatStep Pro drum track:** three pads for `red_pos` 0, 1, 2, a fourth for `invert`, a momentary pad for `red off` (hold = pure black and white). Clock the set to the sequencer's BPM.
- **Defaults:** `low_cut` 0.30, `high_cut` 0.62, `edge` 0.01, `crush` 1.8, `hold` 0.02, `blur` 1 px.

**Phrase structure**

- **Per bar:** `red_pos` steps mids, shadows, mids, highlights.
- **Per phrase (8 or 16 bars):** `invert` once, hue cut to the next red, wedge mask on or off.
- **Build-up:** narrow the red band slowly over 8 to 16 bars and drop `hold` to 0 for maximum flicker.
- **Drop:** on beat 1, snap to wide red (A = 0.18, B = 0.82) for one beat, then back to the poster default.
- **Break:** latch `red off`, raise `edge` to 0.04, drift toward crimson. Red returns on the first kick after.

**Performance tips**

- **Quantise at final resolution.** Any scaling afterwards (slice scaling, LED processor, a downscaled NDI sender) blends the three colours into pinks. Match the composition to the wall's pixel map 1:1.
- **NDI subsamples chroma** and smears red edges. Send the raw image and quantise on the receiving machine. Spout on one machine is lossless.
- **Coarse walls:** on a 6 to 10 mm pitch, 1 px chatter looks like broken pixels. Use `hold` 0.02 to 0.04, blur 2 px, or the `outline` toggle.
- **Heavy red** bleeds in camera and can moiré. Avoid 1 px red lines on coarse walls and run the wall at 60 to 80 % brightness.
- **Wedge angles** must be computed in aspect-corrected pixel space, or the wedge squashes on 3:1 walls.
- **Cost** is negligible. Most of it is in your sources.

## Variations

1. **Beat-gated red.** Red appears only on the kick. Between hits the image is pure black and white.
2. **Wedge mask.** A rotating wedge or circle, after Lissitzky, decides where red is allowed, instead of the picture's tones. Rotate one turn per 4 or 8 bars, or snap 45° per beat.
3. **Palette drift.** The red hue walks ±10° over 32 bars while black and white stay locked. A second accent can swap in for a breakdown.
4. **Kruger layer.** White-on-red caption bars with bold condensed type over the crushed image, text from a MIDI-keyed list.
5. **Spot colour mode.** Black and white with red only in the top band of luminance, the *Sin City* reading.

## Did you know?

- Print tritones are a real colour-mode term next to duotone and quadtone, set up through the same Duotone dialog in Photoshop ([O'Reilly](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/real-world-print/9780321637598/9780321637598_ch09lev2sec13.html)).
- The musical tritone is exactly 600 cents in equal temperament, half an octave ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritone)).
- Resolume only imports `.cube` LUTs ([Resolume](https://resolume.com/support/de/lut)).
