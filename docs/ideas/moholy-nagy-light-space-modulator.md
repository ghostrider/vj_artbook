# Moholy-Nagy Light-Space Modulator

> A turning sculpture of metal and glass, lit by many bulbs. The art is not the object. It is the shadow field on the wall.

![Photograph of the Light-Space Modulator sculpture, 1930](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/50/Light-space-modulator-1930.jpg)
*Photograph of the Light-Space Modulator sculpture, 1930. Image: [László Moholy-Nagy (as given on Commons; source WikiArt)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Light-space-modulator-1930.jpg), [Public domain](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/).*

## The idea

László Moholy-Nagy sketched a light-and-motion machine in 1922 and built it in 1928–1930 with István Seboek. It was shown at the Deutscher Werkbund exhibition in Paris in summer 1930. Its first title was *Light Prop for an Electric Stage*. The name *Light-Space Modulator* came later ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_Moholy-Nagy)).

The machine is a rotating base plate with upright sail-like and round discs of different metals and a glass spiral. More than seventy bulbs, some coloured, light it. The reflective and translucent parts throw moving patterns of light and shadow into the room ([Bauhaus-Archiv](https://www.bauhaus.de/en/discover/objects/lichtrequisit-einer-elektrischen-buehne-reconstruction-of-the-original)). The sculpture is only a light source and a shadow caster. The real image is the projection on the walls.

That is why it works on a club screen. Hard black, white and grey geometry, sweeping and crossing, is easy to read from the back of a dark room. It is also an old idea: Moholy-Nagy's photograms, made from summer 1922, treat light itself as the material ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_Moholy-Nagy)). This chapter builds the **Lumen Rotor**, an original virtual sculpture inspired by the Light-Space Modulator. It is not a replica.

## Origins and references

- **Light Prop for an Electric Stage / Light-Space Modulator (1930).** The original is in the Busch-Reisinger Museum at Harvard, donated by his widow Sybil in 1956 ([greg.org](https://greg.org/archive/2010/08/09/so-many-light-space-modulators.html)). Harvard calls it the most important early example of light and kinetic art ([metaLAB](https://metalab.harvard.edu/p/light-prop)). It is still operated in gallery activations ([Harvard Art Museums](https://harvardartmuseums.org/article/em-light-prop-for-an-electric-stage-em-past-and-present)).
- ***Ein Lichtspiel schwarz weiss grau* (1930).** Moholy-Nagy's abstract film of the machine, premiered in Berlin in March 1930 ([Bauhaus-Archiv](https://www.bauhaus.de/en/discover/objects/lichtrequisit-einer-elektrischen-buehne-reconstruction-of-the-original)). It adds superimposition, lens fragmentation and a short negative sequence to the raw footage ([Coulthart](https://www.johncoulthart.com/feuilleton/2015/06/27/lichtspiel-schwarz-weiss-grau-a-film-by-laszlo-moholy-nagy/)). It runs about 5.5 minutes ([LABoral](https://laboralcentrodearte.org/en/artworks/ein-lichtspiel-schwarz-weiss-grau-1930-2/)).
- **Replicas.** Nan Piene had copies built in 1970 (by Woodie Flowers). Tate Modern and the Busch-Reisinger arranged a 2006 replica built by Juergen Steger ([greg.org](https://greg.org/archive/2010/08/09/so-many-light-space-modulators.html)). The Bauhaus-Archiv Berlin holds a 1974 reconstruction.
- **Harvard metaLAB "Light Prop" (2016, Leonardo paper 2017).** It documents the object with archives, drawings, CAD and 3D renderings ([metaLAB](https://metalab.harvard.edu/p/light-prop)).
- **Later Space Modulators** (B-10 1942, Papmac 1943, Space Modulator 1939–45) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_Moholy-Nagy)).
- **Digital descendant:** "Photonic Light Space Modulator", Glow Eindhoven 2016 (see below).

## Visual language

- **Colour:** black, white and grey. Colour comes only from a few bulbs.
- **Shadows:** raking shadows of spirals, discs and grids that sweep, stretch and cross each other.
- **Motion:** slow, continuous rotation, with flicker from the bulbs.
- **Edges:** hard-edged geometry whose shadows bend as they cross surfaces. In the film, superimposition and negative flashes break the real object into abstraction ([Coulthart](https://www.johncoulthart.com/feuilleton/2015/06/27/lichtspiel-schwarz-weiss-grau-a-film-by-laszlo-moholy-nagy/)).
- **Scale:** reviewers of replicas call the live object "precious and underwhelming" ([greg.org](https://greg.org/archive/2010/08/09/so-many-light-space-modulators.html)). On stage it needs size and darkness.

Picture a big pale wall in a dark room. Over it moves a field of hard shadows. Grids of round holes orbit and stretch. Comb-like bars sweep sideways and squeeze shut as the blade turns edge-on. The thin line of a glass spiral seems to pour outwards. A ring swells into an ellipse and folds flat. Sixteen small bulbs light every part at once, so each part throws sixteen shadows, and where they overlap you get a whole scale of greys. Bright splashes cross the wall whenever a polished blade faces a bulb.

The Lumen Rotor has five parts, each turning at its own rate:

- **Sieve:** a perforated steel disc, tilted so its shadow is an ellipse.
- **Comb:** a slotted blade. Its shadow narrows to a line when edge-on. This is the most Moholy-like motion.
- **Helix:** a glass spiral, half transparent, with a bright caustic line.
- **Halo:** a ring with three spokes, whose tilt precesses over 8 bars.
- **Cage:** six vertical rods orbiting the axis.

## Code design

The audience never sees the machine. The shader treats every wall pixel as a point and asks, for each light, whether the ray to that light passes through a part of the rotor. Each part is a 2D signed-distance mask in its own plane. Transmission is multiplied over parts and summed over 16 bulbs, with penumbra width taken from similar triangles, so parts near the wall are sharp and parts near the bulbs are blurry. This needs one fragment pass, with no shadow maps. Glints are made by mirroring the lights across polished parts. The glass caustic is a faked focusing line. Light state is a 16-step bitmask, each bulb with a fast attack and a slow release. Rotation phase is accumulated on the host (`t += speed/barLength · dt`), never `TIME · speed`, so speed changes never jump.

Render order: wall light field (P1), photogram ping-pong (P2: `max(lit, prev · decay)`), composite with lens fragmentation, negative and post (P3).

=== "Resolume Wire"
    Build P1 to P3 as a Wire patch and load it as a source in a layer. Resolume documents ISF only inside Wire, through the ISF node ([Resolume ISF](https://resolume.com/support/en/isf)), so the custom shader belongs there.

    - **Clock:** BPM/transport to bar phase, an accumulator loop for `t`. Check what your Wire version calls these nodes.
    - **Bulb sequencer:** beat clock divided by 4, a counter wrapping at 16, one of four stored 16-bit patterns, AND with the first N bits from the `bulbs` fader, sent as `lightMask`.
    - **Envelopes:** kick trigger to an envelope (attack 1 ms, release 120 ms). Snare to a 1/16 gate for the negative.
    - **ISF node "lumen_rotor":** P1 as texture A. A second node with `mirror = -1` gives Rotor B.
    - **Composite:** A and B into a Multiply or Lighten blend, split into a feedback loop with a Max blend for the photogram, then Screen, a fragment ISF, invert by `negative`, and a grain and vignette ISF.
    - **Exposed:** spin, bulbs, throw, soft, colour, print, superAmt, toggles for corner and superimpose, triggers for negative and fragment.

    In Arena, load the Wire source on layers over a solid #050505 layer. A second instance with inverted spin, in Multiply or Lighten, is Rotor B. Footage or type above the rotor can use its luminance as a luma mask, so it only appears where the virtual bulbs reach. If you have no shader, render 8 or 16 bar loops from Godot or Unreal and play two at different speeds in Multiply.

=== "Unreal Engine 5.7"
    Two routes.

    - **Route A, real geometry and real lights.** Build the rotor as Static Meshes in a Blueprint with Rotating Movement. Sieve and Comb use Masked materials with an opacity-mask texture. The glass Helix uses dithered opacity so it casts a partial shadow. Use 12 to 16 movable Spot Lights with a small Source Radius as the `soft` control. Fake the glints with extra Spot Lights at mirrored positions and a Light Function material as the part's mask. Light functions work on Movable or Stationary lights ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-light-functions-in-unreal-engine)). The same trick draws the caustic line.
    - **Route B, post-process port.** A Post Process material with a Custom node runs P1 as written. The photogram uses two render targets written by `Draw Material to Render Target`.
    - **Control.** A Remote Control preset exposes spin, bulbs, throw, soft, colour, print, superAmt, negative and lightMask. Remote Control supports OSC, MIDI and DMX through its protocol plugins ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/remote-control-protocols-in-unreal-engine)).
    - **Post:** manual exposure (or the kick flash pumps the image), Bloom 0.6 with threshold 1.0, Film Grain 0.03, Vignette 0.25. Output by Spout plugin or NDI.

=== "GODOT"
    - **2D build:** a `ColorRect` in a `SubViewport` with a `canvas_item` shader for P1. A second `SubViewport` with `render_target_clear_mode` set to Never does the photogram max-blend. A final `ColorRect` does P3. Accumulate `t` in `_process(delta)`. Godot shaders support int uniforms and arrays, so the bitmask trick is optional.
    - **3D build:** rotor meshes under pivots, alpha-scissor materials for holes, 12 to 16 `SpotLight3D` with shadows. `light_size` gives soft penumbras through PCSS, which costs more above 0, so use it on 2 to 4 bulbs only ([Godot Light3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_light3d.html)). Raise the positional shadow atlas size.
    - **Glints and caustic in 3D:** a `SpotLight3D` with a `light_projector` texture. Projectors need `shadow_enabled` and only work in Forward+ and Mobile ([Godot Light3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_light3d.html)).
    - **Post:** `WorldEnvironment` glow with HDR threshold 1.0, plus grain, weave and negative in a final canvas shader. Control with `InputEventMIDI`. Output by NDI, or a community Spout addon if one exists for your version.

## On stage

**Audio reactivity.** Rotation stays tempo-locked. Audio only changes the light. A machine that jitters with the music stops reading as a machine.

| band | drives |
|------|--------|
| kick | all bulbs flash on (1.5x), decay 120 ms, shadows snap hard |
| bass | intensity of the four coloured bulbs, only when colour > 0 |
| snare | negative flash, 1/16 note |
| mids | Halo tilt of about ±10° |
| hi-hats | random 25 % of bulbs off for one frame, caustic gain 0.8 to 1.4x |

**Tempo.** The Comb does one turn per bar. The other parts run at non-whole ratios (Sieve 0.5, Helix -0.25, Cage 0.75), so the full figure repeats only every 8 bars. A `ratchet` amount blends smooth rotation into a stepping, mechanical feel.

**MIDI.** Suggested mapping for KeyStep 37 / BeatStep Pro:

| parameter | what it does | control |
|-----------|--------------|---------|
| spin | speed and direction, -2 to 2 turns/bar | KeyStep knob, BPM-locked; shift freezes |
| bulbs | 1 to 16 active, one hard shadow to a soft grey field | fader; BeatStep Pro sequencer sends the step mask |
| throw | light distance 2.0 to 4.0, near = huge warped shadows | fader, automated in builds |
| soft | bulb radius, razor to smoky | knob |
| colour | black-white-grey to coloured bulbs and shadows | fader, 1 on the drop |
| print | photogram trails | knob; pad clears the print |

Pads: negative (snare), lens fragment (holds one bar), superimpose toggle, corner toggle.

**Song structure.**

- **Verse:** monochrome, one rotor, slow dolly into the wall.
- **Build (last 8 bars):** throw shrinks from 3.0 to 2.0, the bulb chase doubles, colour rises to 0.6, ratchet to 1.
- **Drop:** hard cut. Rotor B in Multiply at 100 %, corner fold on, spin doubled for 4 bars, colour 1, three-strip lens fragment for the first bar only.
- **Break:** a single bulb, soft shadows at minimum, the Sieve alone at one turn per 8 bars, photogram on at decay 0.995 so the holes draw slow arcs.

**Layering.**

- The wall field is the image, not a layer over one. Glints and caustic go on with Add.
- Two rotors: Multiply for piled-up shadows, Lighten for the film's double exposure. Do not Add two full rotors, because the wall white clips.
- Photogram in Screen at 0 to 80 %, tinted cyanotype or white.
- As a matte: put footage or type above the rotor and mask it with the shadow field. Inverted, it only shows in the shadows.
- Negative is a hard 1/16 note flash, never a fade.

**Tips.**

- Move the camera. A slow dolly (scale 1.0 to 1.35 over 16 bars) keeps the room from feeling static.
- Keep the average frame level under 45 % on big LED walls. Hold black at #050505 and cap wall white at about 88 %.
- Keep projected holes at least 4 LED pixels wide, or they moire against the pixel grid.
- Normalise light intensity by the number of active bulbs, or the wall doubles in brightness when a chase opens up.
- Clear the photogram now and then, or the frame turns white at high decay.
- Do not add chromatic aberration. Colour must come only from the bulbs.
- On very wide strips, use two or three rotors side by side with different phases.

### Real-world rotor variant

Build a small, cheap object, light it hard, and film the wall, not the object.

- **Object (30–60 cm tall):** a vertical rod on a slow motor (a mirror-ball motor or a geared turntable, 1–5 rpm). Hang on it a perforated steel disc, an aluminium blade with sawn slots, a laser-cut clear acrylic spiral, a wire ring on a pivot, and 4–6 thin rods. Mount the parts at different angles so a turn changes the shadow shape and not just its position.
- **Lights:** 6–16 small, point-like LED sources. Size matters more than power: penumbra on the wall is about source size × (object to wall) / (light to object). A 5 mm source 0.5 m from the object with the wall 2 m behind gives about 20 mm of penumbra. Add 2–4 coloured LEDs for coloured shadows. Switch them in steps from a DMX LED controller or a microcontroller clocked by the BeatStep Pro.
- **Screen:** a matte white wall or a paper or fabric sheet 1.5–3 m wide. Black out the rest of the room.
- **Camera:** aimed at the screen only, locked off, with manual exposure, focus and white balance. Set the shutter to a multiple of the mains period (1/50 or 1/100 s on 50 Hz), so PWM LEDs do not roll bars through the image.
- **Into Resolume:** a USB webcam appears in the Sources tab. HDMI or SDI cameras need a capture device. Expect about 60–120 ms of latency ([Resolume Live Inputs](https://resolume.com/article/49)). Delay the beat triggers sent to the lights, or accept that the flicker trails the audio.
- **In Arena:** live input, then Levels to crush it to black-white-grey, optional Invert on snare, a feedback duplicate for the photogram, and use it as a luma mask. Physical shadows multiplied with the virtual Rotor A look best: real dust and wobble, virtual precision.
- **Safety:** LEDs only, no hot bulbs near paper. Secure rotating parts and keep the motor slow, because thin metal blades cut.

## Variations

1. **Shadow-only:** never show the object, only the projected shadow field, as the real device is meant to be seen.
2. **Two or three modulators superimposed,** counter-rotating, with the film's negative flash on the snare.
3. **Colour bulbs as RGB:** coloured lights cast RGB-separated shadows that overlap into additive colours.
4. **Audio-driven geometry:** the disc shapes morph with the spectrum, for example a spiral that unwinds on the bass.
5. **Photogram mode:** freeze the shadow as a cyan or white-on-black print and let it decay.

## Did you know?

- The name "Light-Space Modulator" is a later name. The 1930 title was *Light Prop for an Electric Stage* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_Moholy-Nagy)).
- The device uses more than seventy bulbs, some coloured ([Bauhaus-Archiv](https://www.bauhaus.de/en/discover/objects/lichtrequisit-einer-elektrischen-buehne-reconstruction-of-the-original)).
- The 2006 replica was authorised on the condition that it was "not to be considered a work of art" ([greg.org](https://greg.org/archive/2010/08/09/so-many-light-space-modulators.html)).
- The film premiered in March 1930 in Berlin, a few months before the Paris show ([Bauhaus-Archiv](https://www.bauhaus.de/en/discover/objects/lichtrequisit-einer-elektrischen-buehne-reconstruction-of-the-original)).
