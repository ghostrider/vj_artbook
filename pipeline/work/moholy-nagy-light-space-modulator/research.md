# Moholy-Nagy Light-Space Modulator — research

## Origin
László Moholy-Nagy (1895–1946) sketched ideas for a light-and-motion machine in 1922 and built it in 1928–1930 with István Seboek. It was shown at the Deutscher Werkbund exhibition in Paris in summer 1930. It was first called *Light Prop for an Electric Stage* (Lichtrequisit einer elektrischen Bühne). The name *Light-Space Modulator* came later ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_Moholy-Nagy)). Moholy-Nagy taught at the Bauhaus from 1923 and left in 1928 for Berlin, where he worked on composing space with light ([Bauhaus-Archiv](https://www.bauhaus.de/en/discover/objects/lichtrequisit-einer-elektrischen-buehne-reconstruction-of-the-original)). His photograms, made with light-sensitive paper from summer 1922, are the same idea on paper: light as the material ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_Moholy-Nagy)). The original is in the Busch-Reisinger Museum at Harvard, donated by his widow Sybil in 1956 ([greg.org](https://greg.org/archive/2010/08/09/so-many-light-space-modulators.html)). Harvard calls it the most important early example of light and kinetic art ([metaLAB](https://metalab.harvard.edu/p/light-prop)).

## Key works and artists
- **Light Prop for an Electric Stage / Light-Space Modulator (1930)**, Busch-Reisinger Museum, Harvard. It is still operated in gallery activations ([Harvard Art Museums](https://harvardartmuseums.org/article/em-light-prop-for-an-electric-stage-em-past-and-present)).
- **Ein Lichtspiel schwarz weiss grau (Lightplay: Black White Gray), 1930.** Moholy-Nagy's abstract film of the machine. It premiered in Berlin in March 1930 ([Bauhaus-Archiv](https://www.bauhaus.de/en/discover/objects/lichtrequisit-einer-elektrischen-buehne-reconstruction-of-the-original)). It adds superimposition, lens fragmentation and a short negative sequence to the raw footage ([Coulthart](https://www.johncoulthart.com/feuilleton/2015/06/27/lichtspiel-schwarz-weiss-grau-a-film-by-laszlo-moholy-nagy/)). It runs about 5.5 minutes ([LABoral](https://laboralcentrodearte.org/en/artworks/ein-lichtspiel-schwarz-weiss-grau-1930-2/)).
- **Replicas.** In 1970 Nan Piene had copies built (by Woodie Flowers). Tate Modern and the Busch-Reisinger arranged a 2006 replica built by Juergen Steger ([greg.org](https://greg.org/archive/2010/08/09/so-many-light-space-modulators.html)). The Bauhaus-Archiv Berlin holds a 1974 reconstruction ([Bauhaus-Archiv](https://www.bauhaus.de/en/discover/objects/lichtrequisit-einer-elektrischen-buehne-reconstruction-of-the-original)).
- **Harvard metaLAB "Light Prop" project (2016, Leonardo paper 2017)**. It documents the object with archives, drawings, CAD and 3D renderings ([metaLAB](https://metalab.harvard.edu/p/light-prop)).
- **Later Space Modulators** (B-10 1942, Papmac 1943, Space Modulator 1939–45) are related works ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_Moholy-Nagy)).
- **Digital descendant:** "Photonic Light Space Modulator", Glow Eindhoven 2016 (see Visual references).

## How it works
The machine is a rotating circular base plate carrying upright sail-like and round discs of different metals and a glass spiral. Steel, aluminium, glass, plexiglass, wood and an electric motor are the materials. More than seventy light bulbs, some coloured, light it. The reflective and translucent parts throw moving patterns of light and shadow into the room ([Bauhaus-Archiv](https://www.bauhaus.de/en/discover/objects/lichtrequisit-einer-elektrischen-buehne-reconstruction-of-the-original)). The Bauhaus-Archiv gives the size as 201 x 75 x 75 cm. A second description has a metal box with a circular opening, with multicoloured bulbs that flicker on and off. Without the back wall the light can be projected onto a backdrop of any size ([Socks-Studio](https://socks-studio.com/2014/01/18/light-prop-for-an-electric-stage-by-laszlo-moholy-nagy-1929-1930/)). The sculpture is only a light source and shadow caster. The real image is the projection on the walls.

## Visual language
- Black, white and grey, with colour only from the bulbs.
- Raking shadows of spirals, discs and grids that sweep, stretch and cross each other.
- Slow continuous rotation, with flicker from the bulbs.
- Hard-edged geometry whose shadows bend as they cross surfaces. In the film, superimposition and negative flashes break the real object into abstraction ([Coulthart](https://www.johncoulthart.com/feuilleton/2015/06/27/lichtspiel-schwarz-weiss-grau-a-film-by-laszlo-moholy-nagy/)).
- Reviewers of replicas find the live object "precious and underwhelming", so the effect needs scale and darkness ([greg.org](https://greg.org/archive/2010/08/09/so-many-light-space-modulators.html)).

## Live potential
These are design ideas, not claims from sources.
- **Virtual rig:** a rotating stack of metal, glass and perforated plates in a 3D engine (Unreal 5.7) lit by several moving lights. Shadows fall on walls, and the walls are what the audience sees.
- **Kick:** a bulb-flicker burst, or a light-angle jump. **Bass:** brightness of the colour bulbs. **Hi-hats:** fast shutter flicker, or inverted negative frames as in the film.
- **BPM:** lock rotation to one turn per bar or per 8 bars. **MIDI faders:** rotation speed and direction, light elevation, number of active bulbs, grey vs. colour, shadow softness.
- **Layering:** works well as a monochrome luma matte, or as a multiply or screen layer over footage, feedback or typography. Shadows can be used as a mask.
- **Boring on a big screen if:** the speed is constant, the camera is static, and only the object is shown instead of its projection. Add camera moves, multiple overlapped projections, and sudden negative or superimposed cuts.

## Variations
1. **Shadow-only:** never show the object, only the projected shadow field on a wall, in the way the real device is meant to be seen.
2. **Two or three modulators superimposed,** counter-rotating, with the film's negative flash on the snare.
3. **Colour bulbs as RGB:** coloured lights cast RGB-separated shadows that overlap into additive colours.
4. **Audio-driven geometry:** the disc shapes morph with the spectrum (a spiral that unwinds on the bass).
5. **Photogram mode:** freeze the shadow as a cyan or white-on-black print and let it decay, like a photogram.

## Facts
- The name "Light-Space Modulator" is a later name. The 1930 title was *Light Prop for an Electric Stage* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_Moholy-Nagy)).
- The device uses more than seventy bulbs, some coloured ([Bauhaus-Archiv](https://www.bauhaus.de/en/discover/objects/lichtrequisit-einer-elektrischen-buehne-reconstruction-of-the-original)).
- The 2006 replica was authorised on the condition that it was "not to be considered a work of art" ([greg.org](https://greg.org/archive/2010/08/09/so-many-light-space-modulators.html)).
- The film was premiered in March 1930 in Berlin, a few months before the Paris show ([Bauhaus-Archiv](https://www.bauhaus.de/en/discover/objects/lichtrequisit-einer-elektrischen-buehne-reconstruction-of-the-original)).
- Moholy-Nagy coined the term "Neues Sehen" (New Vision) in 1925 and 1932 books ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_Moholy-Nagy)).
- Wikipedia has no separate article for the machine itself; the Harvard article, Bauhaus-Archiv record and metaLAB page are the best primary sources.
