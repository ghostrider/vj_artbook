# Fischinger Visual Music — research

## Origin
Oskar Fischinger was born in Gelnhausen, Germany, in June 1900. He turned to abstract film in 1921 after seeing Walther Ruttmann's *Lichtspiel Opus 1*, and he moved to Munich as a full-time filmmaker ([CVM biography](https://www.centerforvisualmusic.org/Fischinger/OFBio.htm)). His early work included Wax Experiments and Spirals, and the multi-projector *Raumlichtkunst* shows of about 1926 to 1927 (same source). From 1929 he made the *Studies*, a series of black-and-white films synchronized to music (same source). Studies 1 to 12 were distributed internationally. The early Studies were synced to Electrola records, which makes them arguably the first music videos ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Fischinger)). Paramount brought him to Hollywood in 1936. He worked briefly at MGM and Disney, then painted and built the Lumigraph until his death on 31 January 1967 ([CVM biography](https://www.centerforvisualmusic.org/Fischinger/OFBio.htm)).

## Key works and artists
- **Studie Nr. 7 (1931)**: one of the black-and-white Studies synced to music, with an excerpt on CVM's Vimeo channel ([CVM filmography](https://www.centerforvisualmusic.org/Fischinger/Filmography.htm)).
- **Kreise (1933–34)** and **Komposition in Blau (1935)**: color work made with the Gaspar process ([CVM biography](https://www.centerforvisualmusic.org/Fischinger/OFBio.htm), [CVM filmography](https://www.centerforvisualmusic.org/Fischinger/Filmography.htm)).
- **An Optical Poem (1937, MGM)**: paper circles, triangles and rectangles hung on very fine fishing line, shot one frame at a time and moved about a millimeter per frame, set to Liszt's Hungarian Rhapsody No. 2 ([TCM](https://www.tcm.com/articles/246892/an-optical-poem)).
- **Allegretto (1936–43)**: it began as *Radio Dynamics*, synced to a Ralph Rainger tune for a Paramount project. Fischinger bought it back and remade it in color ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Fischinger)).
- **Radio Dynamics (1942)**: a silent film he made in secret, so the rhythm comes purely from the image ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Fischinger)).
- **Motion Painting No. 1 (1947)**: oil on acrylic glass, filmed one brushstroke at a time over about nine months. It is about 11 minutes long and set to Bach's Brandenburg Concerto No. 3 ([Visual Music Archive](https://visualmusicarchive.org/works/w/motion-painting-no-1.html)).
- **Lumigraph**: a live instrument, covered under Variations and Live potential ([CVM](https://centerforvisualmusic.org/Fischinger/Lumigraph.htm)).
- **Fantasia (1940)**: he designed the Bach Toccata and Fugue sequence, then left when Disney made his designs more representational ([CVM biography](https://www.centerforvisualmusic.org/Fischinger/OFBio.htm)).

## How it works
Fischinger's films are built on one rule: every shape change is placed on a musical event, frame by frame. The shapes are simple, so the eye reads the sync rather than the content. CVM's Process page shows graphic notated scores used to plan the animation of *An American March* ([CVM Process](https://www.centerforvisualmusic.org/Fischinger/OFProcess.htm)). That is the same job a MIDI note map does for a VJ. Because the shapes are physical, the motion is also physical. In *An Optical Poem* they float, sway slightly and cast shadows for depth ([TCM](https://www.tcm.com/articles/246892/an-optical-poem)). A digital version has to add that softness on purpose.

## Visual language
- Pure geometry: circles, squares, bars, spirals and staffs, in black and white (Studies) or flat saturated color (*Kreise*, *An Optical Poem*, *Allegretto*) ([CVM filmography](https://www.centerforvisualmusic.org/Fischinger/Filmography.htm)).
- Shapes pop out of black on the beat, then drift or recede in depth. *An Optical Poem* grew from a sequence at the end of *Composition in Blue* in which circles move toward the viewer ([TCM](https://www.tcm.com/articles/246892/an-optical-poem)).
- Layers move at different speeds and sizes, so they read as separate voices, much like instruments.
- The drawn work (Studies, *Motion Painting*) has a hand-made line: freehand figures with no compass or ruler ([Visual Music Archive](https://visualmusicarchive.org/works/w/motion-painting-no-1.html)).

## Live potential
This is my own design reasoning, built on the facts above.
- **Kick** spawns or pulses a big circle. **Snare or clap** fires a bar or square. **Hi-hats** make small dots or ticks, and they work best at low opacity.
- **MIDI notes** map to shape type or screen position, like a score. Pitch gives height, velocity gives size.
- **BPM** sets how long shapes live: easing in time with beat divisions, a new layer every bar.
- **Faders** control layer count, palette, depth or blur, and trails.
- **Layering**: it is mostly black with flat shapes, so it composites well under or over other material with additive or screen blend.
- **Boring risks**: all shapes on every hit with the same easing, a flat 2D look, and a fixed palette. Fix these with sparse triggering, rests (silence is part of the language), depth and shadow, and a shape vocabulary that develops over a set.

## Variations
- **Score view**: a scrolling graphic score, like Fischinger's notated plans, that is also the sequencer.
- **Hanging paper mode**: shapes with a slight sway and soft shadows, after the suspended-cutout setup of *An Optical Poem*.
- **Radio Dynamics mode**: silent rhythm only, where the visuals set the tempo and the audio follows via a bar counter.
- **Lumigraph light-sheet mode**: draw with a hand or controller in a thin slit of light, with the color controlled by a second player or a fader ([CVM Lumigraph](https://centerforvisualmusic.org/Fischinger/Lumigraph.htm)). It suits a two-person performance.
- **Paint-build mode**: accumulating strokes that cover and overlay, after *Motion Painting*, reset every N bars.

## Facts
- Fischinger first met Ruttmann in 1921 and licensed him his wax-slicing machine, which Ruttmann used for backgrounds in *The Adventures of Prince Achmed* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Fischinger)).
- The Lumigraph has no keyboard. Lights sit in a frame that emits a thin slit, so only objects moving into the sheet are seen. Four color wheels are changed by a second player pulling "color control ropes." Patent in 1955, shows from 1951 ([CVM Lumigraph](https://centerforvisualmusic.org/Fischinger/Lumigraph.htm)).
- *Motion Painting No. 1* won the Grand Prix at the Brussels experimental film competition in 1949 ([CVM biography](https://www.centerforvisualmusic.org/Fischinger/OFBio.htm)).
- CVM warns that all YouTube versions of *An Optical Poem* are faded and many are stretched ([CVM filmography](https://www.centerforvisualmusic.org/Fischinger/Filmography.htm)).
- *Radio Dynamics* (1942) is silent. Fischinger made it in secret after being pressed to make a film with sound ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Fischinger)).
- The year of *An Optical Poem* varies by source: TCM says 1937 and notes production around 1936 ([TCM](https://www.tcm.com/articles/246892/an-optical-poem)).
