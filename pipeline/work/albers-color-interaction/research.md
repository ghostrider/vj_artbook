# Albers Color Interaction — research

## Origin
Josef Albers (born 1888 in Bottrop, died 1976 in New Haven) taught at the Bauhaus from 1920, joined the faculty in 1922 and left Germany after the school closed in 1933. He then headed the painting programme at Black Mountain College until 1949 and ran Yale's design department from 1950 ([Wikipedia: Josef Albers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Albers)). His colour course grew at Black Mountain and reached full form at Yale. It centres on watching how colours behave in context, not on colour systems. The book *Interaction of Color* (Yale University Press, 1963) "crowned thirty years of effort" ([Albers Foundation](https://www.albersfoundation.org/alberses/teaching/interaction-of-color)). The 1963 edition was a silkscreen edition with 150 plates; a 1971 paperback carried 10 studies ([Yale News](https://news.yale.edu/2013/07/25/art-book-ipad-app-josef-albers-classic-work-undergoes-magical-transformation)).

The effect he teaches, simultaneous contrast, is older than Albers. Chevreul's 1839 book on it is a standard reference, and earlier observations go back to Ibn al-Haytham, Goethe and Müller ([Wikipedia: Simultaneous contrast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_contrast)).

## Key works and artists
- **Interaction of Color (1963)**: exercises plus about 200 reproductions, mostly made by his students, with Albers' commentary ([Albers Foundation](https://www.albersfoundation.org/alberses/teaching/interaction-of-color)).
- **Homage to the Square (1950-1976)**: over 1,000 paintings of three or four nested squares, oil on Masonite, paint straight from the tube applied with a palette knife ([Wikipedia: Homage to the Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homage_to_the_Square)). Some colour combinations make squares seem to project outward rather than recede (same page).
- **Interaction of Color app (2013)**: Yale University Press, the Albers Foundation and the studio Potion. It has over 125 interactive studies you can pull apart like paper cutouts, plus a colour palette tool ([Communication Arts](https://www.commarts.com/project/22915/interaction-of-color)).
- **Complete Digital Edition (2022)**: a Yale website with 122 interactive plates ([Albers Foundation](https://www.albersfoundation.org/news/yale-press-launches-the-interaction-of-color-complete-digital-edition)).
- **HTTS (Homage to the Square)**: an algorithmic rendering by Cassiel (Nick Rothwell) that builds an evolving sequence of squares from Albers' colour-interaction theories. It was commissioned by Studio Wayne McGregor with the Albers Foundation ([Cassiel](https://cassiel.com/project/htts)).
- **AutoAlbers**: a JavaScript canvas project that makes a new Homage every 12 seconds ([GitHub](https://www.github.com/y-a-v-a/autoalbers)).

## How it works
Albers' claim is that a colour is almost never seen as it really is, so colour is the most relative medium in art. His method puts experience before theory ([The Marginalian](https://www.themarginalian.org/index.php/2013/08/16/interaction-of-color-josef-albers-50th-anniversary/)). The classic exercises:
- **One colour looks like two**: two squares cut from the same paper look different on different grounds.
- **Two colours look like one**: very different colours are made to look alike or nearly alike.
- **Afterimage**: stare at yellow circles, look at a blank square and you see a contrast reversal ([The Marginalian](https://www.themarginalian.org/index.php/2013/08/16/interaction-of-color-josef-albers-50th-anniversary/)).
- **Bezold effect, transparency, spatial illusions**: covered in the foundation's four short videos ([Albers Foundation](https://albersfoundation.org/alberses/teaching/josef-albers/logic-magic-of-color)).

Mechanism: neurons with inhibitory links to their neighbours (V4 is suggested) may play a part. Researchers still debate how much is physiological and how much psychological. The effect likely helps edge detection ([Wikipedia: Simultaneous contrast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_contrast)).

## Visual language
Flat, unmixed colour, hard edges, a strict nested-square schema. The margin under each square is smaller than the space above it, and a white frame surrounds the squares ([Wikipedia: Homage to the Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homage_to_the_Square)). Albers called squares useful because they do not occur in nature and "sit", which lets colour stand out. The same palette in a different order gives a different picture. Rhythm comes from colour steps, not movement: a swap of one colour changes how all its neighbours read.

## Live potential
These are design ideas, not claims from sources.
- **Kick**: swap the ground colour of the outer square while the inner colour stays fixed, so the inner square seems to change without changing.
- **Bass**: slow luminance or hue push on one layer, so the nested squares appear to advance or recede.
- **Hi-hats**: tiny steps of one palette entry, or flicker of a thin border line.
- **BPM / clock**: palette rotation every bar, a new Homage every 8 or 16 beats (AutoAlbers uses a fixed 12 s).
- **MIDI faders**: hue offset, saturation, number of squares (3 to 4 to 16), ground-versus-figure contrast, afterimage gap.
- **Layering**: flat colour fields work as a base under feedback, particles or type. Blend modes will break the pure-colour effect, so keep Albers layers opaque and use them as the bottom layer or a mask.
- **Boring risk**: static colour on a big screen. The fix is a strong change (a swap) timed to the music, with held pauses so the illusion has time to work.
- **Afterimage trick**: a flash to a blank field after a saturated hold gives viewers a real complementary ghost, which needs a hold of several seconds.

## Variations
1. **One colour, two lives**: the same middle colour held fixed while the ground flips on each kick.
2. **Palette from a camera or a Spout feed**: sample colours from another layer and re-order them in the Albers schema.
3. **Grid of Homages**: dozens of small nested-square cells, each with its own palette, swapped in waves across the grid.
4. **Afterimage mode**: hold, then flash to neutral grey on the drop.
5. **Bezold mode**: a fixed line pattern whose colour changes with a single colour added across it (see the Bezold effect video in the foundation list).

## Facts
- The Homage to the Square series ran from 1950 to 1976 and has over 1,000 works ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homage_to_the_Square)).
- Albers painted on the rough side of Masonite panels with at least six coats of white primer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homage_to_the_Square)).
- Most plates in the book were made by his students ([Albers Foundation](https://www.albersfoundation.org/alberses/teaching/interaction-of-color)).
- The 2013 iPad app had more than 75,000 downloads ([Communication Arts](https://www.commarts.com/project/22915/interaction-of-color)).
- The Foundation made four short videos in 2012 that pair audio from Albers' 1966 lecture "The Logic and Magic of Color" with animated plates ([Albers Foundation](https://albersfoundation.org/alberses/teaching/josef-albers/logic-magic-of-color)).
- Simultaneous contrast is thought to enhance edges in the visual field ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_contrast)).
