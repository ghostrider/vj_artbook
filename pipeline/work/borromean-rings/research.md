# Borromean Rings — research

## Origin
- The name comes from the three linked circles on the coat of arms of the Borromeo family (northern Italy), but the motif is far older: the valknut (three interlaced triangles) appears on 7th-century Norse image stones, and a lost 13th-century French manuscript showed the rings as a Trinity symbol (reproduced by Didron in 1843). Peter Tait's 1876 knot catalogue was the first knot-theory work to include them. The Ballantine beer logo gave them the nickname "Ballantine rings" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borromean_rings)).
- Modern mathematical icon: John Sullivan designed the logo of the International Mathematical Union (adopted 2006, announced at ICM 2006 in Madrid on 22 Aug 2006). It shows the "tight" Borromean rings seen along the 3-fold axis ([IMU logo history](https://www.mathunion.org/outreach/imu-logo-history), [IMU logo page](https://www.mathunion.org/outreach/logos/borromean-rings)).
- Sculpture lineage: Tom and Koos Verhoeff build mitered-beam Borromean sculptures in three families ([Bridges 2015](https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2015/bridges2015-53.pdf)).

## Key works and artists
- **Gunn and Sullivan, "The Borromean Rings: A Video about the New IMU Logo"** (Bridges Leeuwarden 2008, pp. 63-70). A 5-minute computer-graphics video that premiered at the ICM 2006 opening ceremony ([Bridges 2008 page](https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2008/bridges2008-63.html), [IMU](https://www.mathunion.org/outreach/imu-logo-history)). The paper describes the scenes: Buckminster Fuller's jitterbug (octahedron to cuboctahedron) whose traced golden rectangles form the rings; real-world photos of Borromean rings; several ring shapes incl. a Möbius-energy minimiser ending in the tight form; woven-rope texture, transparent tubes around a thin core, and a "dissolve" that removes two components; the end fades to the logo ([paper PDF](https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2008/bridges2008-63.pdf)).
- **Tom and Koos Verhoeff**, mitered Borromean sculptures: hexagonal rings (incl. octahedral "Trinity II"), cube weavings with right-angled zigzag beams ("Trinity III" as a single band of non-convex octagons), and a closed triangular braid giving three identical loops ([Bridges 2015](https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2015/bridges2015-53.pdf)).
- **John Robinson**, sculptures Creation, Intuition, Genesis, cited in the Verhoeff paper ([Bridges 2015](https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2015/bridges2015-53.pdf)).
- **Numberphile, "Borromean Olympic Rings"** (2012): explainer video on Borromean rings and their extension to odd numbers of rings ([rosetta.to archive](https://rosetta.to/u/numberphile/borromean-olympic-rings-numberphile), channel confirmed via YouTube oEmbed).

## How it works
- Three loops; no two are linked, but all three cannot be separated. Cut any one and the other two fall free (a Brunnian link) ([Bridges 2008 PDF](https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2008/bridges2008-63.pdf)).
- Perfectly circular Borromean rings are impossible in 3D (Freedman and Skora); ellipses of tiny eccentricity work ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borromean_rings)). Standard picture: three mutually perpendicular golden rectangles inscribed in an icosahedron (Wikipedia, same page).
- The "tight" version is the shape in thick rope with minimal length. It has pyritohedral symmetry (24 elements, 3-fold axis along (1,1,1)), is built of 42 smooth pieces, and is under 0.1% shorter than one made of unit-circle arcs ([Bridges 2008 PDF](https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2008/bridges2008-63.pdf)).
- Verhoeff cube weavings: an odd number of strands per set gives a Brunnian link, an even number gives linked orthogonal pairs ([Bridges 2015](https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2015/bridges2015-53.pdf)).
- Topology facts: crossing number 6, stick number 9, hyperbolic, complement splits into two ideal regular octahedra ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borromean_rings)).

## Visual language
Three interlocking loops in mutually perpendicular planes, 3-fold symmetry along one axis (the logo view, a triskelion-like hexagon of crossings). Over/under alternation at six crossings is the whole drama. Treatments seen in the Gunn/Sullivan video: woven rope, glass-like tubes around a thin core, and rings dissolving away ([Bridges 2008 PDF](https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2008/bridges2008-63.pdf)). Verhoeff sculptures give a hard, faceted alternative: straight mitered beams, hexagons, zigzag cube weavings.

## Live potential
(Design suggestions, not sourced claims.)
- Kick: ring pulse or scale/emission hit; bass: tube thickness or slow rotation speed around the 3-fold axis; hi-hats: crossing sparkle or ring shimmer.
- BPM: lock rotation to a bar (a 120 degree turn around the (1,1,1) axis maps the figure onto itself, a natural loop point).
- MIDI faders: roundness vs. ellipse/hexagon morph, tube radius, camera field of view (narrow = flat logo view, wide = deep 3D), number of rings (3 to 5 odd Brunnian variants).
- Drop: "cut" one ring so the other two fall apart, then rewind; this is the core idea of the link.
- Layering: render with alpha as a Spout/NDI source; works over feedback, grids or particles; the logo view is strong as a flat graphic layer.
- Boring risks: a plain rotating wireframe; needs material, light and a narrative (link, cut, relink).

## Variations
- Morph between families: circles/ellipses, Verhoeff hexagons, cube zigzag weave, triangular braid.
- Jitterbug motion (octahedron to cuboctahedron) that traces the rings, after the Gunn/Sullivan video.
- Dissolve/cut on the drop: one ring unravels and the other two drift apart.
- Odd-number Brunnian links (5, 7 rings) stepping up with the energy of the set.
- Faceted beam rendering vs. glassy tube rendering, crossfaded by a fader.

## Facts
- Freedman and Skora proved no Brunnian link can be exactly circular ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borromean_rings)).
- The IMU logo video was rendered with over 5,000 RenderMan frames from the authors' jReality Java scene graph and assembled in Final Cut Pro; a stereo 3D version for theatres was also made ([Bridges 2008 PDF](https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2008/bridges2008-63.pdf)).
- Hyperbolic volume of the complement is about 7.3277 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borromean_rings)).
- IMU logo competition started 2004; logo adopted 2006 ([IMU](https://www.mathunion.org/outreach/imu-logo-history)).
- Verhoeff: single-band cube weaving yields "Trinity III" ([Bridges 2015](https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2015/bridges2015-53.pdf)).
- An interactive Wolfram Demonstration of the IMU rings (variable tube diameter, icosahedron overlay) exists ([Wolfram](https://www.wolframcloud.com/objects/demonstrations/BorromeanRingsLogoOfTheInternationalMathematicalUnionIMU-source.nb)).
